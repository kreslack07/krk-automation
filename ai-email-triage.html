AI Email Triage in Gmail

The average AU knowledge worker spends 2.5 hours/day on email. Most of that is triage — figuring out what's urgent, what can wait, what needs a reply. Here's how an n8n + OpenRouter workflow cuts that in half.

The 3 triage actions

  1. Auto-label every incoming email with one of: urgent, awaiting-reply, fyi, spam-likely, personal
  2. Auto-summarise the email body into a single sentence, prefixed to the subject line in Gmail
  3. Auto-draft a reply for emails that need one, saved to Drafts for your review (never auto-sent)

The 4-node n8n workflow

  1. Gmail Trigger — new email received (poll every 5 min)
  2. LLM call — send the email to OpenRouter (gpt-4o-mini) with a prompt asking for label + 1-sentence summary + suggested reply (or "no reply needed")
  3. Parse — extract the three fields from the JSON response
  4. Apply — Gmail node: apply the label, modify the subject to include the summary, optionally create a draft reply

The prompt

You are an email triage assistant. For the email below, output JSON with:
- label: one of [urgent, awaiting-reply, fyi, spam-likely, personal]
- summary: one sentence (max 20 words) capturing the actionable core
- suggested_reply: a 2-4 sentence reply if the email requires a response, or null if not

Email:
From: {{from}}
Subject: {{subject}}
Body: {{body}}

Cost

For 50 emails/day, this is roughly A$0.30/day in OpenRouter tokens. About A$9/month. Saves 1+ hour/day of triage time. Payback: same week.

Privacy & compliance

Your email content is sent to OpenRouter for the LLM call. OpenRouter's privacy policy says they don't train on API traffic. If that's not enough, swap OpenRouter for a self-hosted LLM (Ollama + Mistral 7B works fine for triage). Slightly worse at nuance, fully local.

The catch

The LLM can mislabel. Treat the first 2 weeks as calibration — read the labels, correct the ones that are wrong, and adjust the prompt. After 50 corrections, the LLM usually hits 95%+ accuracy on your specific email patterns.
